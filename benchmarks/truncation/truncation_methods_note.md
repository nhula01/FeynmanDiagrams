# Partial sums, exact references, label count and runtimes

## Stated orders

The diagrammatic series for a steady-state observable is asymptotic, so no single order is best at every coupling. Every diagrammatic result is therefore reported as the partial sum through a stated order: N <= 16 for the single Kerr cavity, N <= 10 for the three-site ring, N <= 8 (K = 1) and N <= 6 (K = 3) for the counting cumulants of Table V, and N <= 6 for the eight-site chain. For ratios such as g2 = <a^dag a^dag a a>/<a^dag a>^2 and c_n/c_1 the ratio is formed from the partial sums through order N. `series.py` (`series_record`) stores, for every observable, the partial sums S_0, ..., S_Nmax, the reported order and value and, where an exact result exists, the error at every order; the records are collected in `truncation_results.json`.

## Errors at fixed order

Driven Kerr cavity (Delta = -kappa, eta = kappa, orders to N = 16): the order-16 error is 4.3e-12 at U = 0.05 kappa and 1.8e-7 at U = 0.1 kappa, and is still decreasing there. At U = 0.2 kappa the error is smallest at N = 9 (1.9e-2) and at N = 8 is 6.1e-2, while N = 16 gives 0.37. At U = 0.3 kappa the smallest error is 0.16 at N = 5, against 1.3 for mean-field theory.

Three-site ring (Delta = -kappa, J = 0.4 kappa, eta = kappa; orders 0 to 10 of n, n2 and n3 at 13 values of U): the tenth-order error of g2 is 1.7e-7 at U = 0.1 kappa, 1.7e-5 at 0.15 kappa and 3.9e-3 at 0.3 kappa. From U of about 0.2 kappa a lower order is sometimes more accurate (at U = 0.3 kappa the fourth-order error of g2 is 9.5e-4); for g3 a lower order is more accurate than the tenth from U = 0.175 kappa.

Counting cumulants of Table V: for K = 1 at U = 0.05 kappa the eighth-order errors are 8.2e-6 on the Fano factor and 1.5e-4 on c3/c1; at U = 0.10 kappa they are 7.3e-3 and 0.17 (7.4 per cent, against 24.7 per cent for the Gaussian model). For K = 3 at U = 0.05 kappa the error of c3/c1 falls through every order computed: 0.087 at N <= 3, 0.040 at N <= 4 and 7.1e-3 at N <= 6, where the Fano factor is accurate to 3.4e-4. Against a laboratory-basis reference, or with the recursion missing the terms described below, the errors at N <= 3 and N <= 4 would appear in the reverse order.

## Corrections that affect the counting statistics

Three corrections were needed before the counting statistics could be compared with exact results.

First, the tilted-eigenvalue recursion in `fcs.theta_series` and `fcs_multi.TiltedChain.theta_series` omitted the Rayleigh-Schroedinger terms -sum_{k=1}^{N-1} theta_k psi_{N-k}. With these terms restored, the order-8 value of theta(chi) for K = 1 agrees with exact diagonalization to 7e-8, compared with 3e-6 without them. The omission first matters at order 4 for K = 1 and at order 3 for K > 1. It leaves c1 unchanged and shifts c2 and c3. For the eight-site chain at maxdeg 5, the corrected sixth-order values of c3/c1 are 1.0820, 1.1962, 1.3632, 1.6174 and 2.0134 at U = 0.02 to 0.10 kappa. The uncorrected recursion gives 1.0822, 1.1991, 1.3754, 1.6542 and 2.1043. At every U the shift in c3/c1 exceeds the sixth-order increment |S_6 - S_5|, by a factor of 74 at U = 0.02 kappa that falls to 1.7 at U = 0.10 kappa. At N = 3 the shift in c3/c1 is 2.5e-2 at U = 0.1 kappa.

Second, the cumulants are now obtained from theta(chi) by a discrete Cauchy integral on a circle |chi| = r with M = 16 or 32 points, not by five-point finite differences with h = 0.05. For the exact K = 1 cavity at U = 0.10 kappa, the leading eigenvalue of the tilted Liouvillian nearly crosses the next one at chi of about +0.2: the gap falls to 0.13 kappa, which limits the radius of convergence. The circle radius must stay below this. With r = 0.03 to 0.08 the cumulant ratios agree to 1e-8. At r = 0.15 the value of c3/c1 is already off by 3.6e-4, and at r = 0.2 the contour result is meaningless. The diagram partial sums have no such singularity, and their values at r = 0.1 and 0.2 agree to all printed digits. For the Gaussian row we freeze alpha, n and m at their untilted HFB values and diagonalize the resulting linear tilted Liouvillian. Its theta(chi) is analytic, and the contour values agree with real-axis finite differences extrapolated in h (h = 0.05 to 0.00625) to 1e-6.

Third, the exact references themselves were not converged. In the laboratory Fock basis the truncation error falls off only as the amplitude of the top level, not its population. At U = 0 the ring steady state is exactly coherent, yet with 7 levels per site the laboratory-basis solver gives g2 - 1 = -4.5e-5 and g3 - 1 = -5.6e-4. The cached reference `ring_exactU.json` contains the same error. At U = 0.15 kappa it is 7.4e-4 in g2 and 1.0e-2 in g3. We now solve for the steady state in the displaced frame a_j = alpha_j + b_j, with alpha_j either the linear or the self-consistent mean-field displacement, and truncate only the b modes. This is exact at U = 0 with any cutoff. We use 8 b-levels per site, and the change from 7 levels is at most 1e-6 in g2 for U <= 0.15 kappa and 3e-4 at U = 0.3 kappa. For g3 the change is 8e-6 at 0.15 kappa and 5e-3 at 0.3 kappa. For the three-site counting problem of Table V, laboratory cutoffs [9,6,6] and [11,7,7] give c3/c1 = 1.6037 and 1.6358. The displaced frame with b-cutoffs [8,5,5] to [9,6,6] converges to c3/c1 = 1.63925, stable to 6e-6. A laboratory-basis value of 1.6116 is therefore low by 0.028, mostly because of the cutoff.

## Label count

A label is a normal-ordered monomial in the 2K operators b_q and b_q^dag. Each application of the quartic Kerr vertex followed by the propagator changes its degree by +2, +1, 0, -1 or -2, and only labels of degree 0 contribute to the steady state. Consider an observable of degree d0 carried to order N. After s vertices, a label can still reach degree 0 only if its degree is at most 2(N - s), and it cannot exceed d0 + 2s. The largest degree ever needed is therefore

D_max = max_s min(d0 + 2s, 2(N - s)) = 2 floor(N/2 + d0/4).

The number of labels is bounded by the number of monomials of degree at most D_max in 2K operators, C(2K + D_max, D_max), which grows as (2K)^D_max / D_max!. For the on-site g2 numerator (d0 = 4) the label count therefore grows as K^4 at N = 2 and 3, as K^6 at N = 4 and 5, and generally as K^{2 floor(N/2) + 2}. For <n> (d0 = 2) it grows as K^2 at N = 2 and as K^4 at N = 3 and 4. On the translation-invariant ring the vertex conserves lattice momentum, and only labels of zero net momentum can reach the steady state. That removes one power of K. We instrumented the momentum engine for K = 3 to 14 and the tensor engine for K = 3 to 24. Apart from the roundoff labels described below, the largest label degree reached equals D_max in every case. For <n> at N = 2 the visited label count equals the bound exactly (435 at K = 14). For the g2 numerator at N = 4 the count is about 40 per cent of the bound (29717 against 74613 at K = 8). The fitted exponents over the largest three K are below the asymptotic values because C(2K + D, D) is still pre-asymptotic there. For the g2 numerator at N = 4 the fitted exponent is 4.5 for all labels and 3.6 for zero-momentum labels; the bound over the same range gives 4.8 and 3.8.

Both engines also retain labels that carry only roundoff. The uncontracted terms of the commutator [H_int, X] cancel identically for normal-ordered products, but the engines form both orderings and subtract them. This leaves labels of degree d + 4 with weight about 1e-17 relative to the rest, which the degree pruning keeps. Restricting the commutator to terms with at least one contraction changes no result by more than 1e-17 relative. In the tensor engine at fourth order it is 5 times faster at K = 14 (6.1 s instead of 30 s). For the three-site ring at tenth order it keeps the momentum engine's memory bounded, whereas the original code grew to 0.6 GB per worker without finishing.

## Runtimes

All runtimes were measured on one physical core of the same shared node (SLURM --cpus-per-task=1, BLAS threads 1). Wall time equals CPU time for every point, so the spread between passes reflects contention for cache and memory bandwidth from other jobs. Load averages were recorded for each point. The values in `fig_ring.pdf` come from the pass at load average 23; a pass at load 80 to 99 was slower by factors of up to 5. Observable and parameters are the on-site g2 of a uniform ring with Delta = -kappa, J = 0.4 kappa, eta = kappa and U = 0.15 kappa.

At second order the tensor engine takes 0.0031 s at K = 4 and 2.1 s at K = 32. Above K = 16 the time grows as K^5.9. An earlier split of the same measurement shows that construction of the vertex tensor dominates there, and that the series evaluation alone grows as K^4.0. At fourth order it takes 30 s at K = 14 and grows as K^6.0. With the cancellation-free commutator this becomes 6.1 s, growing as K^5.9. The momentum engine at second order grows as K^5.7 over K = 3 to 6 (2.4 s at K = 6). The exact steady state by sparse time evolution with 5 levels per site grows by a factor of 45 per added site (0.16 s, 5.4 s and 328 s at K = 2, 3 and 4), and with 4 levels per site by a factor of 25 (1426 s at K = 5).
