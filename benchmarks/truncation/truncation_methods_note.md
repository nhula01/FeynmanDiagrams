# Truncation of the diagrammatic series, error estimates, exact references and runtimes

## Truncation rule

The diagrammatic series for a steady-state observable is asymptotic, so a fixed truncation order cannot be right for every coupling. We therefore use one rule throughout. Given the partial sums S_0, ..., S_Nmax computed for an observable, we take the truncation order N* to be the order at which the successive difference d_N = |S_N - S_{N-1}| is smallest among N = 1, ..., Nmax. If the differences are still decreasing at the highest order computed, N* = Nmax. The quoted value is S_{N*} and its error estimate is delta = d_{N*}. For ratios such as g2 = <a^dag a^dag a a>/<a^dag a>^2 and c_n/c_1, the rule is applied to the ratio of partial sums through order N. The rule is implemented in `truncation.py` (`optimal_truncation`). Every partial-sum sequence, N*, delta and, where an exact result exists, the true error at every order are stored in `truncation_results.json`.

## How well delta tracks the true error

For the driven Kerr cavity (Delta = -kappa, eta = kappa, orders to N = 16), the differences are still decreasing at N = 16 for U = 0.05 kappa and 0.1 kappa. There delta is a conservative bound: delta = 1.6e-11 against a true error of 4.3e-12, and delta = 1.0e-6 against 1.8e-7. At U = 0.2 kappa the rule stops at N* = 8 with delta = 4.1e-2, and the true error is 6.1e-2. The true optimum is N = 9, with error 1.9e-2. At U = 0.3 kappa the differences are smallest already at N* = 2, where delta = 0.25 while the true error is 1.08. The best order would have been N = 5, with error 0.16. Beyond U of about 0.2 kappa the successive difference therefore no longer bounds the error of this series, and we do not use the series there.

For the three-site ring (Delta = -kappa, J = 0.4 kappa, eta = kappa) we computed orders 0 to 10 for n, n2 and n3 at 13 values of U. The exact references are described below. Up to U = 0.125 kappa, delta and the true error agree to within a factor of two for g2. At U = 0.1 kappa, for example, delta = 2e-7 and the true error is 2e-7, both for g2 at N* = 10. From U = 0.15 kappa upward the partial sums approach a limit that differs from the exact value by more than the smallest difference, and delta underestimates the error. For g2 the underestimate is a factor of 4 at U = 0.15 kappa (delta = 1.1e-5, true error 4.6e-5), 7 at U = 0.2 kappa and 12 at U = 0.3 kappa (delta = 1.9e-3, true error 2.4e-2). For g3 the factors are 7, 15 and 27, with delta = 9.9e-3 against a true error of 0.26 at U = 0.3 kappa. The rule selects N* = 10 for g2 up to U = 0.125 kappa, 8 from 0.15 to 0.25 kappa and 7 above; for g3 it selects 10 up to 0.1 kappa and 8 above. Restricted to orders up to 6, the rule selects N* = 6 at every U. The error bars in the revised Figs. 10(a) and 11 are the delta of the order-10 rule, and the stored true errors show where they are too small.

For the counting cumulants of Table V the rule gives N* = Nmax in every case, with delta between 1 and 3 times the true error. For K = 1 at U = 0.05 kappa and N* = 8, the Fano factor carries delta = 1.7e-5 against a true error of 8e-6, and c3/c1 carries delta = 2.4e-4 against 1.5e-4. At U = 0.10 kappa the underestimate is a factor of 2 for the Fano factor (delta = 4.0e-3, true error 7.3e-3) and 3 for c3/c1 (5.5e-2 against 0.17). For K = 3 at U = 0.05 kappa, orders 0 to 6 give N* = 6 for c1, c2/c1 and c3/c1 alike, with delta = 7.2e-4 and 1.0e-2 against true errors of 3.4e-4 and 7.1e-3. The rule does not select N = 3 for the three-site ring. The earlier statement that N <= 3 beats N <= 4 at K = 3 came from the numerical errors discussed below and does not survive their correction: the true errors of c3/c1 are 0.087 at N <= 3 and 0.040 at N <= 4.

## Corrections that affect the counting statistics

Three corrections were needed before the rule could be tested on the counting statistics.

First, the tilted-eigenvalue recursion in `fcs.theta_series` and `fcs_multi.TiltedChain.theta_series` omitted the Rayleigh-Schroedinger terms -sum_{k=1}^{N-1} theta_k psi_{N-k}. With these terms restored, the order-8 value of theta(chi) for K = 1 agrees with exact diagonalization to 7e-8, compared with 3e-6 without them. The omission first matters at order 4 for K = 1 and at order 3 for K > 1. It leaves c1 unchanged and shifts c2 and c3. For the eight-site chain at maxdeg 5, the corrected N* = 6 values of c3/c1 are 1.0820, 1.1962, 1.3632, 1.6174 and 2.0134 at U = 0.02 to 0.10 kappa. The uncorrected recursion gives 1.0822, 1.1991, 1.3754, 1.6542 and 2.1043. At every U the shift in c3/c1 exceeds delta, by a factor of 74 at U = 0.02 kappa that falls to 1.7 at U = 0.10 kappa. At N = 3 the shift in c3/c1 is 2.5e-2 at U = 0.1 kappa.

Second, the cumulants are now obtained from theta(chi) by a discrete Cauchy integral on a circle |chi| = r with M = 16 or 32 points, not by five-point finite differences with h = 0.05. For the exact K = 1 cavity at U = 0.10 kappa, the leading eigenvalue of the tilted Liouvillian nearly crosses the next one at chi of about +0.2: the gap falls to 0.13 kappa, which limits the radius of convergence. The circle radius must stay below this. With r = 0.03 to 0.08 the cumulant ratios agree to 1e-8. At r = 0.15 the value of c3/c1 is already off by 3.6e-4, and at r = 0.2 the contour result is meaningless. The diagram partial sums have no such singularity, and their values at r = 0.1 and 0.2 agree to all printed digits. For the Gaussian row we freeze alpha, n and m at their untilted HFB values and diagonalize the resulting linear tilted Liouvillian. Its theta(chi) is analytic, and the contour values agree with real-axis finite differences extrapolated in h (h = 0.05 to 0.00625) to 1e-6.

Third, the exact references themselves were not converged. In the laboratory Fock basis the truncation error falls off only as the amplitude of the top level, not its population. At U = 0 the ring steady state is exactly coherent, yet with 7 levels per site the laboratory-basis solver gives g2 - 1 = -4.5e-5 and g3 - 1 = -5.6e-4. The cached reference `ring_exactU.json` contains the same error. At U = 0.15 kappa it is 7.4e-4 in g2 and 1.0e-2 in g3. We now solve for the steady state in the displaced frame a_j = alpha_j + b_j, with alpha_j either the linear or the self-consistent mean-field displacement, and truncate only the b modes. This is exact at U = 0 with any cutoff. We use 8 b-levels per site, and the change from 7 levels is at most 1e-6 in g2 for U <= 0.15 kappa and 3e-4 at U = 0.3 kappa. For g3 the change is 8e-6 at 0.15 kappa and 5e-3 at 0.3 kappa. For the three-site counting problem of Table V, laboratory cutoffs [9,6,6] and [11,7,7] give c3/c1 = 1.6037 and 1.6358. The displaced frame with b-cutoffs [8,5,5] to [9,6,6] converges to c3/c1 = 1.63925, stable to 6e-6. The value 1.6116 in the submitted Table V was therefore low by 0.028, mostly because of the cutoff.

## Label count

A label is a normal-ordered monomial in the 2K operators b_q and b_q^dag. Each application of the quartic Kerr vertex followed by the propagator changes its degree by +2, +1, 0, -1 or -2, and only labels of degree 0 contribute to the steady state. Consider an observable of degree d0 carried to order N. After s vertices, a label can still reach degree 0 only if its degree is at most 2(N - s), and it cannot exceed d0 + 2s. The largest degree ever needed is therefore

D_max = max_s min(d0 + 2s, 2(N - s)) = 2 floor(N/2 + d0/4).

The number of labels is bounded by the number of monomials of degree at most D_max in 2K operators, C(2K + D_max, D_max), which grows as (2K)^D_max / D_max!. For the on-site g2 numerator (d0 = 4) the label count therefore grows as K^4 at N = 2 and 3, as K^6 at N = 4 and 5, and generally as K^{2 floor(N/2) + 2}. For <n> (d0 = 2) it grows as K^2 at N = 2 and as K^4 at N = 3 and 4. On the translation-invariant ring the vertex conserves lattice momentum, and only labels of zero net momentum can reach the steady state. That removes one power of K. We instrumented the momentum engine for K = 3 to 14 and the tensor engine for K = 3 to 24. Apart from the roundoff labels described below, the largest label degree reached equals D_max in every case. For <n> at N = 2 the visited label count equals the bound exactly (435 at K = 14). For the g2 numerator at N = 4 the count is about 40 per cent of the bound (29717 against 74613 at K = 8). The fitted exponents over the largest three K are below the asymptotic values because C(2K + D, D) is still pre-asymptotic there. For the g2 numerator at N = 4 the fitted exponent is 4.5 for all labels and 3.6 for zero-momentum labels; the bound over the same range gives 4.8 and 3.8.

Both engines also retain labels that carry only roundoff. The uncontracted terms of the commutator [H_int, X] cancel identically for normal-ordered products, but the engines form both orderings and subtract them. This leaves labels of degree d + 4 with weight about 1e-17 relative to the rest, which the degree pruning keeps. Restricting the commutator to terms with at least one contraction changes no result by more than 1e-17 relative. In the tensor engine at fourth order it is 5 times faster at K = 14 (6.1 s instead of 30 s). For the three-site ring at tenth order it keeps the momentum engine's memory bounded, whereas the original code grew to 0.6 GB per worker without finishing.

## Runtimes

All runtimes were measured on one physical core of the same shared node (SLURM --cpus-per-task=1, BLAS threads 1). Wall time equals CPU time for every point, so the spread between passes reflects contention for cache and memory bandwidth from other jobs. Load averages were recorded for each point. The values in `fig_ring.pdf` come from the pass at load average 23; a pass at load 80 to 99 was slower by factors of up to 5. Observable and parameters are the on-site g2 of a uniform ring with Delta = -kappa, J = 0.4 kappa, eta = kappa and U = 0.15 kappa.

At second order the tensor engine takes 0.0031 s at K = 4 and 2.1 s at K = 32. Above K = 16 the time grows as K^5.9. An earlier split of the same measurement shows that construction of the vertex tensor dominates there, and that the series evaluation alone grows as K^4.0. At fourth order it takes 30 s at K = 14 and grows as K^6.0. With the cancellation-free commutator this becomes 6.1 s, growing as K^5.9. The momentum engine at second order, the curve of the submitted figure, grows as K^5.7 over K = 3 to 6 (2.4 s at K = 6). The exact steady state by sparse time evolution with 5 levels per site grows by a factor of 45 per added site (0.16 s, 5.4 s and 328 s at K = 2, 3 and 4), and with 4 levels per site by a factor of 25 (1426 s at K = 5). The submitted text quotes K = 32 in under a second at second order and K = 14 in 40 s at fourth order. On the node as it is now configured we measure 2.1 s and 30 s. An earlier measurement with the same code, taken before the node was rebooted with seven instead of eight DIMMs and at unrecorded load, gave 0.93 s and 15 s. We cannot say how much of the difference comes from the memory configuration.
